# SH2 final gate — sh2-vs-pico-delta lens

*Persisted verbatim. 2026-08-03, final gate before the first irreversible OTP write on the real SeedHammer II. Combined verdict across the three lenses: 1 Critical / 8 Important. NOT safe to write until folded.*

---

## SH2-vs-Pico delta review — final gate before the OTP write

I read the runbook, the quickstart card, the full SH2-mode section of `pico2-bootkey-rehearsal.sh`, all of `platform_sh2.go`, the `Features`/`lock-boot` code in `gui/gui.go`, `sign-firmware.sh`'s output contract, and the rehearsal result. Findings first, then the required delta enumeration.

---

### Findings (most severe first)

**IMPORTANT — `design/RUNBOOK_custom_boot_key.md:358` — Runbook step 6 flashes the *unsigned* image, guaranteeing a boot failure on the sealed SH2.**
Step 5 signs via `sign-firmware.sh`, which by contract never mutates its input and writes `<img>.signed.uf2` (`scripts/sign-firmware.sh:50`; the rehearsal script's own comment at line 530-531 warns "callers must flash the .signed.uf2 it produces, NOT the original"). Step 6 then runs `picotool load --verify $PWD/seedhammerii-$(git rev-parse HEAD).uf2` — the original, whose signature `build-firmware` deliberately zeroes (`picosign sign -clear`, runbook line 43-44). Failure scenario: operator completes a perfect burn, pastes step 6 verbatim → bootrom rejects the unsigned image → operator enters the "If it does NOT boot" box, whose advice ("re-run step 5 and re-flash") re-flashes the same wrong filename forever; next stop is the recovery image and 2am panic. No OTP damage (step 6's box correctly forbids burning another slot), but it is a guaranteed false failure with a debugging loop that never names the cause. The quickstart card (`FIRMWARE-QUICKSTART.txt:283`) gets it right (`${FW%.uf2}.signed.uf2`) — the two documents diverge, and the runbook is the one declared authoritative. Fix: change step 6 to flash `${...}.signed.uf2` and add "confirm you flashed the `.signed.uf2`" as the first item in the not-boot checklist.

**IMPORTANT — `design/RUNBOOK_custom_boot_key.md:362` + `FIRMWARE-QUICKSTART.txt:296` — the boot verdict cannot be observed on laptop power: accepted firmware reboots into BOOTSEL before the LCD ever initializes, indistinguishable from a signature rejection.**
This is a pure SH2-vs-Pico delta the blinky could never show. In `Init()` (`platform_sh2.go:224`), `monitorPowerSupply` runs **before** LCD configuration (line 251); it demands a 20–28 V PD contract (`adjustSupplyVoltage(d, minVoltage=20_000, ...)`, line 431) and on failure calls `rebootIntoBOOTSEL()` (line 439) — by upstream design, precisely for the "connected to a flashing computer" case. A laptop port that can't source 20 V (most can't) makes a *correctly signed, accepted* image show: dark screen, device re-enumerating as `RP2350 Boot` within seconds — the exact signature-rejection signature. Both documents say to expect the "normal startup screen with (UNLOCKED)" immediately after `picotool reboot`, still tethered to the laptop. Failure scenario: operator flashes good firmware → dark screen + BOOTSEL → concludes rejection → re-signs in a loop; flashes the official recovery UF2 → *same symptom* → concludes the recovery path itself is broken → improvises on a machine with two irreversible writes just made. Note this also invalidates any Pico-style "still enumerable = rejected" reasoning (the rehearsal's `bootsel_present` heuristic) for the SH2. Fix: in both step 6 and quickstart §4, add: "after `picotool reboot`, unplug from the computer and power the machine from its normal PD supply before judging boot; on laptop power even good firmware re-enters BOOTSEL by design (`platform_sh2.go:428-441`), so a dark screen there means nothing."

**IMPORTANT — `FIRMWARE-QUICKSTART.txt:184-187` (stability note) — the SH2's USB-PD front end plus the already-observed `error -71` transient are unaddressed inputs to the one unrecoverable window.**
Delta: the Pico 2 hangs directly off 5 V VBUS; the SH2 puts an AP33772S PD sink (`platform_sh2.go:211`) between the USB-C connector and the system, and PD link events (source capability re-advertisement, hard reset → VBUS to vSafe0V) can occur with no firmware involvement while the chip sits in BOOTSEL. The survey of this exact device already logged one `device descriptor read/64, error -71` (EPROTO) during enumeration — a transient class never seen on the Pico. Whether the RP2350's rail actually passes through the PD stage **cannot be verified from this repo** (no schematics); I flag it as unverifiable, but the observed -71 alone justifies action. Failure scenario: PD renegotiation or a repeat of the -71-class fault interrupts `picotool otp load` mid-way through the 16 rows → slot 1 permanently dead (bootrom refuses rewrites of programmed ECC rows). Fix, concrete and cheap: (a) do the burn from a **non-PD 5 V source** — a USB-A port through an A-to-C cable has no PD state machine at all and exactly reproduces the rehearsal's electrical condition; (b) before step (d), replug and re-enumerate until `dmesg` shows a clean enumeration with no `-71`, and run `--sh2-precheck` once more as a ~70-row USB soak immediately prior (it doubles as the mandated re-read of state).

**MINOR — `scripts/pico2-bookey-rehearsal.sh:717-779` (`--sh2-verify-slot` / `--sh2-verify-valid`) — the success paths remain hardware-unproven, but the residual surface is narrow and fail-closed; one pre-burn dry-run would shrink it further.**
Every picotool-parse primitive these wrappers use *has* now met real hardware in its success form: nonzero 16-row `read_slot` succeeded on the SH2 itself (slot 0 inside `sh2_require_seedhammer` during `--sh2-precheck`) and on the Pico (1c/4c); `otp_field` parsing `KEY_VALID=0x3` ran on the Pico in phase 4; `read_row_raw24` on rows 0x04b–0x04d ran on the SH2 in precheck. What has never run for real is only the wrapper glue (arg flow, `reject_rehearsal_key`, ordering), which the 28/28 fake-picotool e2e covers, and any failure there is a `die` costing zero OTP — the operator is stalled at a gate, not misled. Two notes: (1) the operational trap is deleting `rehearsal-work/` between (d) and (e): `reject_rehearsal_key` then dies mid-procedure ("CANNOT CHECK"); the quickstart's "keep rehearsal-work/ until section 3 is COMPLETE" (line 166-167) and the `ALLOW_UNCHECKED_KEY=1` escape cover this — verify the directory still exists *before* step (d), not after. (2) Cheap hardening: on burn day, before step (d), run `--sh2-verify-slot 1 --key ~/.sh2/sh2-boot-key.pem` against the still-pristine device and confirm it dies with exactly `SLOT 1 READBACK MISMATCH` showing all-zeros vs your hash — that exercises the complete wrapper chain on the real device with only the final comparison differing from the post-burn success path (ignore that message's "permanently unusable" text in this context).

**NIT — `gui/gui.go:1624-1630` — the `lock-boot` NFC debug command remains unconditionally live in the fork firmware.**
Not actually a Pico-delta (stock firmware has it too) and it cannot waste a slot (`AddBootKey` returns the existing slot on exact match); the runbook's "never send lock-boot" warning already covers it. Recorded only because it is the sole OTP-write path in the firmware you will run daily.

### Delta enumeration (the lens's five questions)

1. **Starting state — CLEAN, empirically.** Everything readable is now proven equivalent: `--sh2-precheck` on the real unit matched the post-phase-1 Pico in every register that governs the write — `PAGE1_LOCK*`/`PAGE2_LOCK*` (0x040404 pattern on both), `CRIT1` ×8 = 0x000001, `BOOT_FLAGS1` ×3 = 0x000001, slots 1-3 empty across all 16 rows. The factory white-label rows the Pico never had live in different rows (`USB_BOOT_FLAGS`/`WHITE_LABEL_ADDR` around 0x059–0x05c in page 1; strings in the user area ≥ 0x0c0) from both write targets (`BOOT_FLAGS1` = 0x04b–0x04d; `BOOTKEY1` = page 2, 0x090–0x09f); OTP programming is per-row, and the only page-granular mechanism is the lock rows, which the precheck reads directly. That slot 0 was written by the on-device bootrom API (`LockBoot`) rather than picotool leaves identical OTP state — and it is the *state*, not the writer, the next write sees. The one honest residue (already in the runbook): lock rows are necessary-not-sufficient; the definitive writability proof for *this* device is the write itself.
2. **Procedure delta — CLEAN by strict subset.** The SH2 sequence performs exactly two writes, both byte-for-byte proven on the sealed Pico from the same starting values: a single-slot 16-row `otp load` (Pico phase 4), and the *identical command* `otp set -s BOOT_FLAGS1.KEY_VALID 0x2` over a row currently 0x000001 (Pico phase 4, 0x1→0x3). The Pico did strictly more (CRIT1 writes) — nothing the SH2 procedure does is outside the rehearsed set. The genuinely never-exercised SH2-only surfaces are exactly the findings above (verify wrappers' success path, boot-verdict-on-machine-power, `(UNLOCKED)` display).
3. **Firmware — VERIFIED cosmetic-only.** `FeatureSecureBoot` has exactly one consumer in the entire tree: `gui/gui.go:2717-2719`, appending ` (UNLOCKED)` to the version string. Nothing functional is gated; an `isSecureBootEnabled()` error at Init merely leaves the feature unset (`platform_sh2.go:246-249`). Post-burn, `nvalid==2` makes it return false deterministically. The consequential firmware delta is not secure-boot-related at all — it is the power-negotiation reboot (finding 2 above).
4. **Silicon/package — CLEAN, one unverifiable noted.** The SH2 is RP2350**B** (its pinout uses GPIO26–30, `platform_sh2.go:112-127`; GPIO30 does not exist on the A package) while the Pico 2 is RP2350A — same die, same OTP map and write path; picotool's handling is now proven on both physical devices. `boardVersion()` on the SH2 reads the already-burned white-label version string and never consults `PACKAGE_SEL`. Die stepping of the SH2's chip cannot be verified from the repo; no published RP2350 erratum touches the OTP write path, so I judge this immaterial, but it is an assumption, not a proof.
5. **One-shot steps — see the Important finding on PD/-71.** The interruption window itself is unchanged from the Pico; what changed is the plausibility of an interruption (PD front end, one observed USB transient on this exact path). Mitigate to rehearsal-equivalent conditions (non-PD 5 V source) before `otp load`.

### Verdict

The OTP-write core is delta-clean and better-evidenced than any prior round: both irreversible commands are byte-identical to operations proven on sealed silicon from a starting state empirically matched on the target. I found **no Critical**. The three Importants are all in the *post-burn judgment path*, not the burn itself — but two of them (unsigned-image flash in runbook step 6; boot verdict taken on laptop power) will, as written, manufacture a convincing "the burn failed" narrative minutes after a successful burn, which is exactly when operators improvise. Fold all three (two are one-line doc edits, one is a bench-setup instruction) before executing steps 2–3.
