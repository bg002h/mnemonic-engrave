## 2. THE CENTRAL QUESTION — one plan, three parallel branches

**Recommendation: ONE plan, THREE branches run in PARALLEL, one serialised
prerequisite before them and two serialised joins after.**

The evidence, in the order it settles the question:

1. **No build dependency among the three.** Every cross-repo dependency is a
   crates.io pin on a **codec** crate — `mk-cli` takes `md-codec = "0.42.0"`;
   `mnemonic-toolkit` takes `md-codec`, `mk-codec` and `ms-codec` the same way.
   P3 edits **CLI** crates only. No edit in the plan changes a published codec.

2. **The one shared mutable artifact is `mnemonic-io-lib`, and P3 asks NOTHING
   NEW of it.** Measured against the same eleven items P1's boundary table
   weighed: `md` and `mk` adopt `write_private` and nothing else; `mnemonic`
   adopts `remedy::history_purge_block` with its structured half and nothing
   else. Both are already inside P1's scope. **P3 adds zero crate items and
   changes zero crate signatures.** This is the specific serialisation risk the
   brief asked to be named, and the answer is a measurement, not a hope.

3. **They share one PREREQUISITE and it is P1's.** `crates/mnemonic-io-lib` is
   on neither crates.io nor `origin/master` — `git ls-tree -d origin/master
   crates/` lists `crates/me-cli` alone. All three pins wait on the same push
   and SHA. That is one gate **before** the three, not an ordering **among**
   them.

4. **The two real collisions are outside the three repos.** The GUI schema
   mirror is one repo and one branch, with P2 editing its `ms` file and P3 its
   `md`/`mk`/`mnemonic` files — disjoint files, shared branch, which the
   parallel-isolation rule forbids; so it is one commit by one agent. The seven
   journey goldens are regenerated by drivers that resolve `md`, `mk` **and**
   `ms` as absolute paths into each repo's `target/release`, so they need all
   three P3 binaries plus P2's `ms` and are last.

5. **The one ordering that looked real dissolved under measurement.** §10's
   acceptance made `md`'s `--out` look like a prerequisite for `mk`'s
   `--from-md1-set`. Run both ways today, `mk encode` fed a **grouped** md1 set
   and an **ungrouped** one produced **byte-identical** `mk1` output at exit 0,
   because `mk` strips display separators on intake. So the new flag need only
   skip non-`md1` lines, which makes it era-agnostic and buildable first.

**What would change the recommendation**, stated in the plan so a reviewer can
attack it: if `mnemonic`'s pre-parser scanner were ruled into the shared crate
rather than the toolkit; if the operator reversed §4's exemption and gave
`md`/`mk` the argv refusal; or if the world-readable write gate were ruled into
P3. Any of the three makes the crate shared mutable ground and the answer
becomes three sequential phases.

## 3. WHAT I MEASURED THAT CONTRADICTED THE SPEC OR THE BRIEF

Six contradictions, all reproduced by running something rather than reading it.

### 3.1 `mk`'s "2 → 1" is not one code — it is three out of one match arm

**The most consequential finding, and it is a defect in a GREEN spec's normative
ruling.** `CliError::exit_code` sends `Codec(_)` and `MdCodec(_)` to 2 through a
single arm, and both of §6f's distinct table columns come out of it:
`mk decode <garbage>` → 2 and `mk repair <BCH-uncorrectable card>` → 2. §6f
gives `md`, `ms` and `mnemonic` a repair-uncorrectable of **2** and an invalid
artifact of **1**; `md` gets that split from an explicit `Ok(2)` bypass whose own
comment says why. `mk` has no bypass, so the one-line edit §6f implies breaks a
parity three other CLIs hold — and `SetReassemblyMismatch`, the funds fix, also
exits 2 out of the same function and must stay there.

**And the suite would not notice in either direction.** A histogram of `.code(N)`
across `crates/mk-cli/tests` gives 12 sites — four 0, four **2**, three 5, one
64 — and **all four 2s pin `SetReassemblyMismatch`**. Zero tests pin the
invalid-artifact 2 by exit code. Filed as **F-291** and built by the plan's
exit-code entry, which asserts both directions.

### 3.2 `mk encode` DOES put a non-artifact line on stdout

§2's table reads *"non-artifact lines on stdout: none, ever"* for `mk`. Measured
with a two-record key file, `mk encode --keys` writes **5 lines, one of them
blank** — a separator between cards at `crates/mk-cli/src/cmd/encode.rs:339`.
The spec's cell was measured on a single-card run, where the branch cannot fire,
and §10's acceptance runs exactly the `--keys` path that does. `me sysw pack`
tolerates it (blank lines are skipped), so it is a §4 violation rather than a
packability defect. It has its own plan entry.

### 3.3 The brief's flag name was wrong; the spec's was right

The channel is **`--decrypt-password`**, not `--decrypt-passphrase`. All five
named channels exist and all five emit the advisory today — measured one by one,
with `bundle` emitting **two** because its secret channel is
`--slot @N.<subkey>=` and `--passphrase` both, and with three of the five exiting
non-zero for unrelated reasons while the advisory fires regardless.

### 3.4 The cite gate already resolves all three subject repos

The brief predicted it *"almost certainly cannot resolve your three repos"*.
It can, and has since 2026-08-18: `descriptor-mnemonic`, `mnemonic-toolkit` and
`mnemonic-key` are all in `ROOTS`. Verified with a 16-line probe **before**
writing a citation. What actually dangles is **`mnemonic-gui`** — P3's fourth
repo — plus two smaller blind spots the probe found: a leading-dot path loses
its dot to the citation regex, and `.tsv` is not in the extension list. Filed as
**F-296** with the one-line fix and the collision measurement behind it (of the
GUI's 504 tracked files, the only shared paths are four top-level files already
ambiguous across existing roots; `src/schema/` collides with nothing because no
other root has a top-level `src/`).

### 3.5 `md`'s `-` gap is a silent misread, not a clean rejection

§6b describes the gap as verbs that reject `-`. In `md` it is worse:
`md decode -`, `md verify -` and `md inspect -` **accept `-` as a literal md1
string** and fail at exit **1** with `string does not start with HRP md1`, not at
clap's exit 2. **A gate written as "the command fails today" would pass before
and after the fix.** The plan's gate is byte-equality with the positional run.
`mk`'s four reading verbs already read stdin at exit 0, measured with a real
card — the mirror image of P1's finding about `mt`.

### 3.6 P3 touches five repos, not three

`descriptor-mnemonic`, `mnemonic-key`, `mnemonic-toolkit`, plus `mnemonic-gui`
(the schema mirror, in §7's own gate) and `mnemonic-engrave` (the seven
goldens). The brief named three.

**Two further measurements worth recording as negatives.** The
`display-grouping-vectors.tsv` corpus is **byte-identical across four repos**
(one sha256, `7147b0ec…`) and CI-pinned in three of them, and it contains rows
keyed `hyphen` and `comma` — which §6c removes. It **survives untouched**,
because every consumer is codec-level and maps the keyword to a `char` inside
the test, while §6c's removal is at each CLI's `parse_separator`. And the GUI's
drift gate is blind to the `--separator` change for three independent measured
reasons: it scopes itself to `mnemonic`, its choices comparison is scoped to
flags whose pinned JSON carries non-null `choices` (the toolkit reports `null`
at all four carrying subcommands), and its binary comes from a pin far behind
the current CLI.

## 5. CONSULTS

**One consult, one question, `model: "fable"`.**

**Question:** does P3 strip `mnemonic bundle`'s three `#` comment headers and
three blank lines from stdout, or change only its `--group-size` default and
`--separator` vocabulary?

**Facts supplied as given** (measured before dispatch, so the consult spent no
budget re-deriving them): 12 stdout lines, 6 non-artifact; the comments are
redundant with the HRPs on the artifact lines; the governing §4, §6a, §3, §2a,
§7 and §9a text verbatim.

**Verdict: (A) GROUPING ONLY.** The reasoning returned: §6a is the spec's own
operationalisation of §4 and scopes it by an explicit per-verb table covering
`md`/`mk`/`ms`/`mt` only, bound to `encode`, a verb `mnemonic` does not have;
§2a enumerated `mnemonic`'s involvement as exactly two items with no header
item; stripping them breaks a shipped machine-readable surface, the precise
class §6a refused to break for `mk decode` and said *"gets its own phase and its
own gate"*; and §9a's no-tier-movement ruling removes the packability motive
since `mnemonic` never feeds `me sysw pack`.

**Folded as written.** The plan puts it in the out-of-scope list with those
citations, adds a **control assertion** to the grouping entry so an implementer
who reads §4 as absolute goes RED, and files the question as **F-295** with the
counterargument and the flip condition recorded.

**Everything else was settled by running a command**, per the brief — including
the two questions that looked like consults and were not: whether the shared
grouping corpus breaks (it does not; the consumers are codec-level), and whether
`md`'s `--out` must precede `mk`'s `--from-md1-set` (it need not; grouped and
ungrouped md1 produce byte-identical `mk1`).

## 7. WHAT A REVIEWER SHOULD ATTACK FIRST

1. **The `channel::destination` decline.** Named in the plan as its weakest, with
   the flip condition stated. Two lines either way.
2. **The `records::split_record_stream` decline.** It has the most measurement
   behind it and is therefore the one most worth trying to break.
3. **The engraving card's contents.** §6c requires the plan to specify them or
   two implementers render them two ways. The plan follows `ms`'s measured shape
   — plain `label: value` on stderr with the grouped string first — and
   explicitly does not follow `mnemonic bundle`'s `#`-prefixed card. Two
   in-constellation precedents disagree and the plan picked one.
4. **The separator ownership claim.** §7's P2 row assigns *"the whitespace-only
   separator"* to `ms`; §7's P3 row does not mention the separator at all; §6c
   binds it *"everywhere"*. P3 claims `md`/`mk`/`mnemonic`'s separator because no
   other phase can. That is a plan reading a gap in a GREEN spec, and it is the
   kind of reading that should be checked rather than accepted.
5. **The line citations.** F-279 measured that 14 of 15 of P1's went stale under
   P1's own early entries while the gate reported all 15 green. **P3 cites three
   repos, so the exposure is larger.** Every citation here names its symbol
   beside its number, and the plan says in bold to locate by symbol and
   re-measure before quoting. That is a mitigation, not a fix.
