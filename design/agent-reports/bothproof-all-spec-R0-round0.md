## DECISIONS REQUESTED

**Item 5 — identification. Recommendation: OPTION 1 (titles), with two amendments, and NO footer on either side.**

The measured table in C2 removes the premise of the debate. Option 2's claim to "maximum margin" is false: side A with no title has **2.40 mm** of spare, which is *less* than side A with a title (**3.60 mm**), because the title pushes the 5.0 mm block below the screw-hole band and buys back a whole row. So the fragility §5 worries about is already present on side A at its most generous configuration; declining the title does not buy safety, it buys a plate you cannot identify in a drawer. Option 3 (drop 3.4) is unnecessary: side B with a 3.0 mm title has exactly the 2.40 mm side A has with no title at all, so 3.4 mm costs nothing side A does not already cost — and it costs a rung on the one plate whose entire purpose is the rung ladder.

The two amendments:

1. **The title sits at the side's smallest rung (3.8 mm on A, 3.0 mm on B), not at the size of the block it borders.** §2.4's rule as written gives side B a 4.4 mm title and **1.00 mm** of spare, which is under a third of a row and is not defensible. This requires `Fitted` to carry an explicit title size.
2. **No footer on either side.** With a title, a footer overflows side A outright (83.4 mm > 82 mm) and overlaps the last body row on side B (C3). The ALL proofs must therefore pass an empty footer and must not inherit `ftProofFooter` from `ftProofOutcomeFor`.

On the 2.4 mm question specifically: 2.40 mm is acceptable **only if the composition's shape is pinned by a test rather than discovered at engrave time.** The real hazard §5 names — a glyph change dropping `font/constant` from 39 to 31 columns and forcing a fourth row — is not mitigated by a bigger margin, because the failure is discrete (one row, 3.0–5.0 mm) and larger than any margin under discussion. It is mitigated by a test that pins each side's per-block row count and total device-unit height (see the §7.5 restatement above), so a font change fails CI instead of failing a plate. Given that test, 2.40 mm is fine; without it, 5.40 mm would not be.

Make the title text a specimen as well as a label, per RECON §4's own note — `A 5.0+3.8` and `B 4.4+3.4+3.0` (13 characters, verified to fit the inset span at both title sizes in both faces: 26 columns available at 3.8 mm sh, 31+ at 3.0 mm). Note that `EngraveFitted` engraves `Title` VERBATIM (freetext.go:11-19), not through `TitleString`, so these strings must be validated against `MaxTitleLen` and against `TitleFace.Decode` by a test, not by the runtime.

**Item 6 — triggers. Recommendation: the convention break does NOT matter for MATCHING, but `BOTHPROOF!ALLA`/`ALLB` should still be rejected — rename to `FRONTPROOF!` / `BACKPROOF!`.**

Reading `ftProofForTrigger` (freetext_proof.go:437-465): the lookup is `typed == p.Trigger` first, and the suffix path is guarded by `!p.Sizeable || !strings.HasPrefix(...)` and then by an exact match against `strconv.FormatFloat` of a real rung. `"ALLA"` parses as no rung, so the `BOTHPROOF!` entry falls through and the outer loop reaches the exact ALLA entry regardless of ordering. A mistyped `BOTHPROOF!ALAA` matches nothing and stays ordinary text. So the spec's own reasoning is correct: **exact-match lookup does guarantee what the first-character convention was protecting**, and prefix-ness alone is not the defect.

Reject the names for two other reasons the spec did not consider:

- **On-screen disambiguation, not matching, is the real convention.** The comment at freetext_proof.go:482-484 says the prompt names the plan "because the triggers differ ONLY in which faces they prove and the operator has to be able to tell, from the screen, which of them they typed." ALLA and ALLB prove identical faces, so that mechanism yields near-identical prompts (Important finding above). Triggers differing in their fourteenth character, producing prompts differing in nothing, is a worse failure than a mistype that matches nothing — a mistype is refused, a mis-pick is engraved.
- **`BOTHPROOF!` being a strict prefix creates a latent trap.** It is inert today only because the ALL entries would be non-`Sizeable`. That is an invariant nobody has written down, and the `Sizeable` flag is one line away from being copied.

`FRONTPROOF!` and `BACKPROOF!` differ from each other and from all three existing triggers at the first character, neither is a prefix of anything, both are self-describing in the prompt, and both are unreachable today. If the `BOTHPROOF!` family name must be preserved for continuity, use `BOTHPROOF!FRONT` / `BOTHPROOF!BACK` — differing at the eleventh character rather than the fourteenth, and legible in the prompt — and record in the source that the ALL entries must never be marked `Sizeable`. In either case §4's "the prompt must name which side it is loading and say the other exists" has to be implemented as a field on `ftProof` read by `ftProofAsk`/`ftProofReplaces`, not left to `Plan.Name()`.
